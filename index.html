Consistency is my superpower

Nobody hands you strength. You earn it.

In Grade 6, as a rebel kid, I joined the karate academy in our society park. Kumite, the face-off between two opponents, became my favourite. Every bout I lost in practice showed me a weakness to fix. In belt tests, I never lost one.

At one grading, I was double promoted. Sensei Abhinash praised me in front of the whole dojo.

I earned my black belt right before the pandemic shut everything down. In 2024, I walked into a gym and started from zero. People called me too thin, too weak. I kept showing up. Today I do one-arm push-ups, and the one-arm pull-up is next.

Eidi Capital

So I started a finance club

Talking money with friends turned into something bigger. I brought together six to eight girls from my community and started our own finance club. I named it Eidi Capital, after Eidi, the money elders gift us at the Muslim festival of Eid. For most of us, it is the first money we ever get to decide about.

We meet around Eid and other festivals. Everyone brings their Eidi total, and we work it out together: what to spend, what to save and what to invest. We were all under 18, so we could not invest on our own. Instead, we looped in our parents, pitched them our plans, and they helped us put our Eidi into investments like digital gold. The ideas came from us, and our parents helped make them real.

Running it taught me how to lead: planning each session, making sure the quieter girls get their questions answered, and turning a group of friends into a group that plans.
